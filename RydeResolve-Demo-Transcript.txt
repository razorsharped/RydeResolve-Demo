00:00:00.000  A fairer ride
Welcome to RydeResolve, a multi-agent dispute resolution prototype for the Ryde Digital Native challenge.
Riders and drivers can report a problem, inspect the supporting evidence, and understand a recommended outcome.
Rumi, our friendly guide, keeps the experience approachable while the backend handles calculations, policy checks, and audit recording.
This video shows the working interface, the core agents, and the design decisions behind the prototype.

00:00:28.890  The problem and intended value
A ride dispute is rarely just a question about a refund.
Support teams may need to compare trip records, messages, payments, photos, receipts, and two different accounts of what happened.
RydeResolve organizes that information into a shared case, then makes the evidence and policy basis visible.
The intended value is faster standard-case handling, consistent policy application, and explanations that customers can understand.
These benefits would need production evaluation; we are not claiming measured savings or real-world accuracy gains.

00:01:02.835  Four categories · eight examples
The demonstration covers the four categories named in the handbook.
They are Route Deviation, No-Show Charge, Property Damage or Mess, and Safety Incident.
Each category has two examples: one reported by a rider and one reported by a driver.
That gives eight cases, labelled A through H, in the existing case list.
The cases demonstrate refunds, justified fees, cleaning-cost recommendations, and bounded conduct remedies.

00:01:31.333  Report a dispute
To report a dispute, open Submit your dispute at the top of the page.
Choose whether you are reporting as a rider or driver, then select the category you want to report.
The category is independent of the trip selection; changing the trip does not silently replace your choice.
Describe what happened and optionally provide the amount disputed.
If the selected trip lacks the relevant records, the system keeps your category and explains what evidence is still needed.

00:01:59.443  Add supporting evidence
The optional evidence controls accept pictures, receipt images, readable text files, and audio recordings.
There is also a browser control for recording a short audio clip.
The backend validates file types and size limits, extracts text from text files, and records file hashes and metadata.
An upload alone does not establish authenticity or responsibility.
In the current simulated mode, images are received without visual analysis, and audio is received without automatic transcription.
The interface states these limits directly.

00:02:34.946  How the three agents work
The architecture has three core agent roles, supported by an orchestrator.
The Rider Advocate and Driver Advocate run in parallel against the same evidence ledger and applicable policies.
Each presents its position, requested remedy, evidence references, and uncertainties.
The Judge receives both cases and returns a structured ruling with an explanation.
Backend checks validate citations, constrain monetary remedies, and apply a review gate before the recommendation is shown.

00:03:05.293  Case A · refund the excess fare
In Case A, the rider says the driver took a longer route and charged too much.
The backend compares the actual distance with the expected distance and accounts for the allowed traffic adjustment.
The remaining unexplained deviation is twelve point one seven percent, above the example policy threshold.
The calculated fare difference is one dollar and forty-eight cents.
Both advocates are heard, and the judge recommends refunding that verified difference within the illustrative case records.

00:03:34.082  Inspect the decision process
The expandable decision process shows how the result was reached.
It includes the complaint, fare calculation, route adjustment, retrieved policies, both advocates, and the judge's comparison.
These are recorded calculations and decision summaries, not a claim to expose a model's private internal reasoning.
Rumi also answers why the decision was made, how both sides were considered, and what happens next.
Customers can see the main outcome first and inspect the details when they want them.

00:04:04.297  Cases B–D · fair decisions for both sides
A longer route does not automatically mean the driver was wrong.
Case B contains a justified road-closure detour, so the original fare stays the same with no refund.
For pickup disputes, Case C refunds the cancellation fee because the driver did not reach the pickup zone.
Case D supports the driver's report: arrival and a seven-minute wait meet the five-minute waiting rule.
The cancellation fee is therefore not refunded under the example policy.

00:04:32.466  Cases E–F · photo records and receipts
The cleaning cases show why evidence matters more than who files the report.
In Case E, the supplied photo metadata links the spill image to another trip.
The judge rejects the cleaning claim and recommends refunding the already charged thirty dollars.
In Case F, the sample photo records match the trip, the rider acknowledges the spill, and a receipt supports forty dollars in cleaning costs.
The judge approves the documented amount within the example policy cap.
These media records are clearly labelled illustrative demo evidence.

00:05:05.018  Cases G–H · standard conduct complaints
The safety examples focus on corroborated, non-threatening conduct complaints.
Case G uses a sample transcript and the driver's acknowledgement to support the rider's complaint about disrespectful language.
The recommendation is an apology and a five-dollar service credit.
Case H supports a driver's report of abusive rider language and recommends a conduct reminder and avoiding future pairing.
Threats, physical danger, conflicting evidence, or unverified allegations still require human review.
The demo does not impose account sanctions or process payments.

00:05:39.719  Auditability and honest AI status
Technical details expose the evidence ledger, agent responses, policy citations, execution modes, and recorded audit events.
The readiness score is a rule-based signal, not a measured probability that the decision is correct.
The public deployment currently uses simulated structured agent responses.
Evidence calculations, citation checks, monetary limits, and audit recording run in the backend.
A provider adapter supports live model calls, image inputs, and optional audio transcription when configured, but no provider credentials were added for this deployment.

00:06:15.131  Build approach and development-tool tips
The build approach starts with deterministic evidence rules and clear policy boundaries, then adds agent roles around a shared ledger.
The interface was refined to show a simple outcome first and expandable technical detail below.
A useful tip when working with CodeBuddy or WorkBuddy is to request one focused change at a time and specify the expected behavior.
Keep calculations and policy limits separate from generated arguments, then test missing records and conflicting evidence as well as successful cases.
Genuine development conversation screenshots should accompany the submission; this walkthrough does not replace that evidence.
